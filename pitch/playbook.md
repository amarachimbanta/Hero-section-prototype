# Pitching founders: playbook

Skill: `/pitch` · Proof: `pitch/profile.md` · Pitches saved in `pitch/pitches/` · Follows `content/human-writing.md`

## The one rule
**If the message could be sent to another company by changing the name, it's generic. Rewrite it.**

Founders get dozens of AI-written messages a week. They all start with "I came across your company", praise the "mission", list skills, and ask for a call. Mine start with *them*, show I looked at their product, prove I've solved their kind of problem, and offer something small.

## What every pitch has (in this order)
1. **Them, now.** One line about something real and recent: funding, a launch, a milestone, a new market, a hiring push, something the founder posted. With a date. "Congrats on the seed" alone is not enough. Connect it to what it means for design ("a seed round and 3 engineering roles open usually means the product is about to grow faster than the design").
2. **Their product, specifically.** One thing I noticed in their actual product or site, tied to the job. Not a takedown: a gap or a chance. "Your signup asks for company size before showing anything, so people decide before they see the value."
3. **Proof that fits.** One result from `pitch/profile.md` that matches their problem. "At Nexera I took onboarding from hours to under a minute and activation went up 40%."
4. **A small offer.** Something useful and specific: a 3-screen idea for the gap I noticed, a short Loom walkthrough, a quick audit of one flow. Not "let's hop on a call".
5. **An easy ask.** A question they can answer in one line. "Want me to send the 3 screens?" Not "Would you be open to a 30-minute call to discuss synergies?"

## Length by channel
| Channel | Length |
|---|---|
| LinkedIn connection note | 300 characters max. Parts 1, 3 and 5 only. |
| LinkedIn DM / X DM | 60 to 120 words |
| Cold email | Subject under 6 words, about them, lowercase is fine. Body 80 to 150 words. |
| Application form "why us" / cover note | 120 to 200 words. All 5 parts, plus the job's top must-have answered directly. |
| Follow-up 1 (day 4) | 1 to 3 lines. Add something new (the screens, a fresh observation). Never "just bumping this". |
| Follow-up 2 (day 10) | 1 to 2 lines. Graceful close: leave the door open. |

## Research checklist (do before writing)
- **Company:** what they sell, to whom, stage, funding (amount, date, investors), recent launches, press, team size, open roles.
- **Person:** the founder or hiring manager's name, role, and what they've posted or said lately. A line from their own post is the strongest opener there is.
- **Product:** open the site or app. Find one specific thing related to the role. Screenshot it.
- **Signal:** the one "why now" fact. Write the source and date next to it. If nothing recent exists, lead with the product observation instead. **Never invent a signal.**

## Never write
- "I hope this message finds you well", "I came across your company/profile", "I'm reaching out because", "I'm passionate about", "I'd love to", "great fit", "perfect fit", "excited about the opportunity", "touch base", "circle back", "quick call", "leverage my skills", "add value to your team"
- A list of tools or skills
- Years of experience (see `pitch/profile.md`)
- Flattery about the "mission" or "vision" with nothing specific
- Any number that isn't in `pitch/profile.md`
- Attachments in a first message. Link one project, the one that matches.

## Hook options
1. Your product doesn't break all at once. It breaks one button at a time.
2. How many button styles does your product have right now? Count them.
3. AI can make a screen in seconds. It can't keep 50 screens consistent.

## Post
Your product doesn't break all at once. It breaks one button at a time.

A new screen ships with a slightly different blue. Another one gets its own spacing. Someone generates a modal with AI and nobody checks it against the rest.

Six months later there are four kinds of buttons. Users aren't sure what's clickable. Every new feature takes longer because nobody knows which version is the right one.

That mess costs real money, and it's exactly what a design system fixes.

On Nexera, an ERP with Inventory, Procurement and Admin modules, I built the design system in Figma: tokens, variables and reusable components that every module shared. It contributed to a 98% task completion rate on core workflows, because users only had to learn a pattern once.

The business side is simple. Engineers ship faster when they reuse instead of rebuild. Users trust a product that behaves the same everywhere. New features stop starting from zero.

And with AI making screens in seconds, this matters more now. Speed without a system just makes mess faster.

How many button styles does your product have right now?

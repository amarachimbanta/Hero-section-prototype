## Post
This design got rejected.

A warm sign-up page. "Welcome home!" on a torn-paper card. I loved it.

The team didn't, and I had to start from scratch.

It stung. Then it made me ask a better question.

This was an ERP. Admin, inventory, procurement, sales, finance, HR. One product, many departments, all depending on each other.

I was designing a screen. They needed a system.

So I went back to research. How does a company actually move through this product every day? What happens to finance when I change something in procurement? How does the admin see every department without jumping through hoops?

From then on, every decision had to answer one question: does this help the company run without friction?

That rejection made me a better designer than an approval would have.

Has a rejected design ever taught you more than an approved one?

## Post
Most designer portfolios are a theme park.

An intro animation. Scroll effects on everything. Five clicks before you see a single screen.

Fun for the designer. Tiring for the recruiter with a stack of other tabs open.

So I'm rebuilding mine with one rule: someone hiring should find what they came for without riding the roller coaster.

What I'm changing:

1. The work comes first. My story can wait.
2. Every project opens with the problem and the result, before the process.
3. Motion only where it explains something.

A portfolio is a product. Its user is someone busy, deciding whether to talk to you.

If you hire designers: what's the first thing you look for when you open a portfolio?

## Post
A designer sent me a message I haven't stopped thinking about.

He and his team work long hours. Every move they make is monitored. If someone leaves the work group, they get watched. And the pay doesn't come close to matching any of it.

As a designer, this is the part that bothers me most.

Design doesn't happen in the hours someone can see.

The idea that fixes your onboarding shows up on a walk. The layout finally clicks at 11pm, after two days of staring at it. Half the job is thinking, and thinking looks like doing nothing.

So when every minute is tracked, you get designers who look busy. More frames and more files, and less of the one decision that actually moves your numbers.

Micromanagement burns people out first. Then it shows up in the product.

If you lead a design team, judge it by what ships and what it does for your users. Then give people room to think.

You can watch a designer's screen all day. The work that matters is happening in their head.

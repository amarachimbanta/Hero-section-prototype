## Post
QuieTell is done. It took longer than I planned, and I'm glad it did.

It started as an idea about one problem. Cats hide it when something is wrong. They eat a little less, sleep a little more, sit by the window instead of on your lap. Owners notice the changes one at a time, never all together. By the time it adds up, it can be late.

Then life got in the way. There were gaps. Weeks where the Figma file didn't move.

But coming back to it again and again taught me something I didn't expect. This wasn't really a pet app. It was a study of how people make decisions.

A few things kept showing up in the research.

People don't trust their own memory. "When did this start?" is the hardest question to answer when you're worried. So every check-in is kept on record, ready to show the vet what shifted and when.

Every cat has a different normal. So the app learns each cat's baseline over 7 days before it compares anything.

People ask other people before they act. So there's a community, and the App Store screenshot says "You're not the only one wondering."

When the options don't fit, people go quiet. So there's an assistant that already knows the cat and helps you put it into words. It also says "I'm not a vet" before you type a thing.

None of those decisions are about colours or fonts. They're about worry, memory, timing and trust.

That's the part of product design I want to keep doing.

The full case study is on my Behance, and I wrote a longer piece about it (links in the comments).

I'm open to new product design roles. If you're building something where understanding people matters as much as the screens, my DMs are open.

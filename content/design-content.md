**Whose post is it?** If the screenshot says **Amarachi Mbanta**, it's mine. Any other name is someone else's post that moved, and it's there so I can compare mine against it.

## What I'm going for
I'm not selling a service in my posts. I'm showing I'm a designer who understands the business: I make products that convert, keep users and save teams time. Every design post should leave a founder, PM or hiring manager thinking "she gets it, I want her on my product".

So every design post brings **value**, and the value has a **business side**.

## The value rule
Each design post must give the reader at least one of these:
1. **A takeaway they can use tomorrow.** A rule, a check, a pattern. Specific enough to try on their own product.
2. **A business reason.** Why the design decision matters for revenue, sign-ups, conversion, retention, support load, speed to ship, or trust.
3. **A look behind the decision.** What I chose, what I rejected, and why. That's what shows how I think.

And it must answer this, in plain words, somewhere in the post:
> **So what for the business?**

## How to talk business without making things up
- Never invent numbers or results. If there's no data, say what the design is **meant** to move and why: "a visitor decides in about 5 seconds whether to scroll, so the hero has to show the product working, not describe it."
- Use the business words founders use: sign-ups, demos booked, drop-off, churn, support tickets, time to value, trust.
- Tie the detail to the outcome in one sentence: detail, then why it matters for the business.
- If I have real numbers from a project or a client (with permission), use them. They're gold.

## Post shapes that bring value
Add to this list as the swipe log grows.

| Shape | What it looks like | Business angle |
|---|---|---|
| **Decision breakdown** | One design decision, what I rejected, and why | Shows judgement: why this choice helps conversion or trust |
| **Before and after** | Two screens, what changed, why it's better | What the change should do for sign-ups, drop-off or clarity |
| **Teardown** | A well-known product's screen: what it does well, what I'd change | What it teaches other founders about their own product |
| **Rule + proof** | One design rule, shown on my own work | A rule a founder can apply without hiring anyone |
| **Mistake I see a lot** | A common design mistake, why it hurts, the fix | The cost of the mistake (lost users, confused buyers) |

## Mine vs theirs: what's missing
Updated by `/swipe` every time a design post of mine is logged. Compares my design posts to the ones that moved. Top 3 gaps, most important first, each with what they do, what I did (quoted), and the exact fix for my next post.

Last updated 2026-10-15. My 2 design/career posts vs Felix Lee (2) and Ruslan Kosinov (1).

**Good news first.** People talk to me. My founder post got more comments (42) than reactions (39). Felix's best got 102 comments on 607 reactions. My concrete meme (in `content/humor.md`) got more reactions (430) than Felix's post on the same topic (333). The ideas are there. What's missing is how the posts are built.

### 1. Name the thing. My posts stay vague where theirs get specific.
Felix: "1M+ members in 160 countries. Someone in Singapore books a session with a designer in San Francisco." Ruslan: "sat there for maybe a minute."
Me: "In August, there was a difference, and it shows progress." "my progress is quiet but noticeable." What difference? What progress? The reader can't see it, so they can't feel it.
**Fix:** every claim gets one concrete detail. Not "there was progress" but what happened: a final round, a freelance project, a founder who replied. If it's private, describe it without names.

### 2. Every post needs one line people would repost.
Felix got 20 reposts from one line: "Most people are paid to have opinions. Designers are paid to reason." And "It's the only thing that scales."
Mine: my founder post has one ("Not because I pitched him. Because he knew me.") and did better. My job hunt post ends with "Consistency keeps us going!", which people have read a thousand times.
**Fix:** before posting, point to the one line someone would screenshot. If there isn't one, write it. It usually comes right after the turn.

### 3. Give the reader the value, not only my story. Then drop the asks.
Felix's posts are about him but end on a lesson for founders. Ruslan's ends on a stance other designers want to share.
Mine: the job hunt post is all about me, then "ps: Need a product designer to join your team? I'm open to work." The founder post ends with "Found this valuable? Repost ♻ to reach others". Neither Felix nor Ruslan ever asks, and they get 3 to 20 reposts.
**Fix:** end on what the reader (a founder, a designer) should take away, with a business reason where it fits. Put "open to work" in the first comment or the profile banner, not in the post. Stop asking for reposts. A good line earns them.

**Also, the visual has to match the point.** Felix's "FIRED up" image is the milestone. Ruslan's X screenshot is the punchline. My job hunt post shows app screens with no word about them. My founder post shows a Figma file and a cat on a phone. Either explain the work in one line in the post ("the onboarding I designed in August") or use an image that tells the story.

**Big idea from this batch:** Felix wrote the "serious" version of my viral concrete meme. That's a pattern I can use: **post the meme for reach, then a week later post the value version** (why everyone has a design opinion, what founders should do instead, and my quotable line). The meme gets me seen. The value post makes people want to hire me.

## Patterns in design posts that move
- **Show motion as motion.** A motion design posted as still images loses the one thing that makes it special. Always use the video, and point the reader at one moment in it.
- **A designed infographic gets reposts.** Faustine's Figma-vs-production card got 48 reposts on 1,276 reactions. If the image makes the point alone, people share it. Worth building these in HTML for my own posts.
- **Funny, specific details beat general advice:** "an error message written by a backend developer at 2am" says more than "handle errors well".
- **End with an easy question about the reader** when the goal is comments: "What was the first design you were proud of?" beats "What do you think about design systems?" (from Alisha M.'s milestone post, 202 comments on 251 reactions).
Updated once there are 5+ rows. Now 5 posts (3 theirs, 2 mine). Early patterns:
- **Specific beats general.** Every post that moved has numbers, places or a short scene.
- **One quotable line** drives reposts more than any ask does.
- **Take a side.** Felix (design opinions) and Ruslan (AI interviews) both pick a clear side. The comments come from people agreeing or arguing.
- **The visual does a job:** a pun, a punchline, or proof. Not decoration.
- **No asks.** None of the posts that moved ask for a repost or a job.
